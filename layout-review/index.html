What the two-storey house gains

  • One stair. Today the stair appears three times (EG, OG, DG arrival) and the DG foyer alone is 8.6 m². T1 and T4 have one straight flight on the party wall, 1.0 × 4.05 m, 15 risers of 19.3 cm with 27 cm goings for a 2.90 m storey; in T4 the cellar flight lies under it and is entered from a small lobby at the flight's south end.
  • No set-back storey. The DG's 58.5 m² plate yields 47 m² of rooms and is ringed by roof strips; two full storeys put every square metre under a usable ceiling, and under this Bebauungsplan two full storeys on the GRZ footprint use the GFZ exactly (0.25 × 2 = 0.50), so nothing is left on the table.
  • Fewer windows. Nine openings in T1 and ten in T4 instead of fifteen: the dressing, the guest WC, the laundry and the en suite are internal and mechanically ventilated (the Hessische Bauordnung allows this), the kitchen gets one high-sill window, and the bedrooms get one each. The east wall of the ground floor is blind for its front 7.4 m anyway, because the garage stands there.
  • Stacked plumbing. The guest WC, the laundry and the family bath stand over each other on the party-wall side; the en suite, the master bath and the cellar laundry share the east stack.

Option T4 · the plot's maximum: 8.29 × 14.07 m, two storeys plus WU basement

What the maximum is on this plot, in the terms of the BauNVO and the Hessische Bauordnung (HBO). The GRZ of 0.25 (§19 BauNVO) allows 241.65 m² of building on the 966.6 m² plot, 120.8 m² per house; garages and drives were left outside that figure in the approved Bauantrag (BauNVO 1977), so the house itself can use it all. The GFZ of 0.50 (§20 BauNVO) allows 483.3 m² of Geschossfläche, 241.7 m² per house; two full storeys on the GRZ footprint give exactly that, and a basement does not count because it is not a full storey under the HBO (no 2.30 m of clear height over two thirds of its area above the natural ground). The Baufenster runs from 5.00 to 20.00 m behind the street, 15 m deep. The width is fixed by the garages: with a double garage on each side boundary beside the front part of the house (5.41 m on the west, 4.8 to 6.9 m on the east, following the boundary jog) and the party line at s = 13.70 as in both current designs, each house keeps the 8.29 m of today. The depth can grow to 14.07 m: at 14.57 m, which would be the full 120.8 m², the east house's rear corner would stand 2.88 m from the angled east boundary, inside the 3.00 m minimum Abstandsfläche (0.4 H, at least 3.00 m, HBO); at 14.07 m it stands 3.00 m clear. The last 4 m² of GRZ could be won by moving the party line 0.5 m west, which would narrow the west garage to 4.9 m, or by a slight widening at the front only; both are for the Bauamt conversation. The plate therefore is 8.29 × 14.07 m, t = 5.00 to 19.07, mirrored about the party line, both halves at one level (273.40 as in Inhouse 12), with a WU basement under each half because the soil report puts the water at W2.1-E / W2.2-E.

Inspired by the Rötha design Doppelhaus: the offset pair (T4v)

You sent bauWerk Leipzig's design Doppelhaus in Rötha. Read from its plans: two halves of 7.10 m width and 11.00 m depth, offset against each other by 2.50 m, with a 3.00 m single garage at the outer front corner of each; 130.5 m² of Wohnfläche per half, which is 63.0 m² on the ground floor (Flur 4.9, Garderobe 2.0, WC 1.7, HWR 4.3, Kochen 7.6 and a 42.5 m² Wohnen/Essen across the full width behind a fully glazed garden wall) and 67.5 m² upstairs (two 10.75 m² children's rooms at the street, a 9.6 m² Tageslichtflur with the straight stair across the house, a 9.7 m² master bath with a freestanding round tub at the window, a 7.6 m² Ankleide, and a 19.1 m² bedroom across the garden front under a full-width window band). The terrace is a wooden deck that wraps from the garden round the outer side of each half, so the offset gives every terrace a corner sheltered by the neighbour's wall. The elevations show white upper boxes over a dark, largely glazed ground floor, flat roofs, the upper storey projecting over the garden glazing, and the garages as low cubes in front.

What it shares with the Altkönigstraße house is almost everything in the programme: entrance, cloakroom and WC in the front corner, kitchen at the street side, living room across the garden wall, children at the street upstairs and the master suite over the garden. What differs is size (our halves are 8.29 × 14.07 m with 187 m² against their 130 m², and ours carry a fourth bedroom, a second full bath and a basement) and the three design moves that make the Rötha pair look like two houses. Those three transfer as follows. Large glazing is in T4 now: the living room's garden wall is a 3.6 m glass element (3.0 m lift-slide plus a fixed pane) beside the bedroom's 2.2 m window, 5.8 of the 7.71 m of south façade, and the master bedroom and bath above repeat the openings; a full-width band like Rötha's on the upper floor would need the partition between the master bedroom and the master bath to stop at a fixed glass pane, a detail rather than a plan change. The wrap-around terrace works on the rear outer side of each half, behind the garage: on the east half it becomes a private patio for the ground-floor bedroom, on the west half a second terrace for the living room. The offset is the move the Abstandsfläche decides, below. Rötha's daylit stair hall does not transfer, because our stair sits on the party wall; the flat roof gives the landing a rooflight instead. The upper floor projecting over the garden glazing is possible only in the offset version, where the GRZ has 8 m² of slack per house: a 1.0 m cantilever along the 8.29 m garden wall counts as Grundfläche and uses exactly that slack.

How the drawings were checked

Each plan is generated from a list of walls, windows, doors and fixtures in metres. Before rendering, a script tests every drawing: a new partition may not meet a window opening or end within 0.12 m of a reveal; every bath, bedroom, dressing, WC room, corridor and study must have a door on its boundary; every door opening must lie inside a wall; a pocket door's cavity must lie inside a straight wall with no other opening; no fixture may overlap another fixture or a wall; no tall wardrobe may stand in front of a window; no door swing may hit a fixture; and the free depth in front of each WC, basin, shower entry, tub and bed side is measured by ray-casting against walls and fixtures and compared with the VDI 6000 minimum and comfort figures. All six proposals pass; the five faults the script reports are in the current plans. The rendered drawings were then reviewed twice by an independent architectural reviewer against a defect checklist; the first pass found 40 items (among them walls meeting window reveals, pocket cavities crossing wall junctions, a tub standing in front of a door, en-suite doors behind bed heads) and every one was corrected or, where the drawing was right, answered in the captions. The second reviewer pass did not complete (the review service refused the run); the corrections were re-checked by the geometry script and by eye, so that pass remains open.

What this review could not verify

  • Dimensions come from screenshots at ±5 cm. Have the partition positions set out from the as-built survey, not from these numbers.
  • The stair direction was inferred from the arrival-landing logic (a flight cannot arrive flush against the DG north wall). If a flight runs the other way, the only change is that the OG sofa slides 1 m.
  • Roof slopes, parapets and the walkability of the set-back strips are not visible in the plans.
  • The stair is drawn as in your plans, a 2.3–2.5 m straight run, which is too short for a 2.8 m storey; it is kept as a schematic. The flue footprint also differs between your three drawings (0.85 × 1.65 m on the EG, 0.23 × 2.55 m on the OG, 0.24 × 1.6 m on the DG); the proposals keep each as drawn.
  • The research agents could only read search extracts, not full pages; every figure above is tied to the URL that showed it, and VDI 6000-1:2024-07, DIN 18040-2 and Neufert should be checked in the primary text before they go into a tender.
