Benchmark: what good hotels do, and what I took from it

Hotel rooms are the most optimised small suites there are: a brand's standard room has to fit bed, bath, closet and desk into a 3.6 to 4.2 m wide bay, and the chains test every centimetre on thousands of rooms. Eight patterns carry over to a house like this one. The table says where each one landed in the proposals.

Hotel patternWhat it doesUsed here
Entry sequence: door → closet → bath → bed at the windowWet and storage zones sit at the door, the bed gets the daylight and the quiet end. It is the standard bay of almost every chain hotel and the reason their rooms feel bigger than they are.DG loop 1 keeps exactly this order: hall, walk-through closet, bedroom with the bed facing the east window. Hides cables and gives a 'solid wall' feel even where the wall is a partition.Recommended for both DG bedrooms; in loop 1 it is also the feng shui buffer to the bath behind.

Open-plan or glass-walled bathA trend in newer design hotels: the tub or the whole bath opens onto the bedroom, sometimes behind switchable glass. Brings light into the bath but guests complain about privacy, and it only works for a couple.Not adopted. Both DG baths keep a wall. If you like the look, the loop 2 sliding door can be a glass door with a curtain; that gives the light without the downsides.

Sizes behind these: a standard hotel bathroom is about 4.2–5.5 m², a junior suite 36–48 m², and planners treat 3.5 m as the minimum room width; main circulation paths are kept at 0.9 m, 1.2 m where two people pass. Your DG suite at 47 m² is junior-suite territory, which is why it can afford a 10 m² bath and a 9–10 m² closet and still have a 17–18 m² bedroom.

Feng shui check on the loop 1 bedroom

  • Commanding position. Head against the solid west partition (4.67 m long, no window). From the pillow you look across the room at the door in the far north-east corner. The door is neither behind the bed nor in line with the foot.
  • Feet point east, to the east window, not to a door.
  • What sits behind the headboard. On the bath side it is the double basin, not the toilet, which goes on the west party wall. If you want to be strict, give the bed a 0.3 m deep built-in headboard wall with shelving behind; that also hides the cabling for reading lights.
  • Symmetry. Night tables on both sides, and the window is not above the bed.

If you only do one thing: move the closet partition north to the window edge. That single wall buys the bedroom 2.2 m² and costs the closet nothing you will miss. The bath wall is the second step.

Not touched in loop 1: the OG and EG. Their quick fixes are listed under loop 2 and can be done without the rest of loop 2.

What I could not verify

  • Stair direction. I kept the stair and both landings exactly where your drawings have them and assumed about 1 m of landing at each end. If the flight arrives at the south end on the OG, the sofa in the living nook slides 1 m south; nothing else moves.
  • Roof slopes. The DG is drawn as a 7.13 × 8.20 m box. If there are knee walls on the east or south, the low zone is best used for the bed, the dresser and the tub, which is how both DG proposals place them.
  • Chimney. The green element at x 2.8–3.05 m on the OG and DG and the 0.85 × 1.65 m block on the EG read as a flue or shaft. All proposals keep it inside a wall.
  • Door swings and clearances were checked on paper at 0.9 m leaves, 0.8 m in front of basins and toilets, 1.0 m beside beds. Check the final layout with your tool's clearance view before you fix wall positions.
