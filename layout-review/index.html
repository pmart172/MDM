1 · Reading the building

Before any wall moves, four facts about this house decide what is possible.

  • Party wall west, windows north, east and south. The 0.22 m west wall is half of a double-leaf party wall. Every daylit room must sit on the east or south face; the south face looks onto the garden. The south-east corner of each floor is the best room in the house, and on the DG it is currently the smallest one.
  • Stair. Three straight flights on the west wall, all stacked and all climbing north → south: the EG flight starts at y 1.55 m and arrives on the OG south landing; the OG flight starts at the north end (y 1.37 m) and arrives on the DG south landing at y 2.60 m. The 1.0 × 1.0 m zone at the foot of the DG foyer's south wall is that arrival landing. No door may swing onto it, which is why the DG bath door stays in its present opening in all three options and becomes a pocket door.
  • Flue. The green element in your drawing is a flue or shaft. It runs in the 0.24 m wall between the DG foyer and the north-east room, stands free in the OG landing, and sits in the 0.85 × 1.65 m block on the EG. Every proposal keeps that wall and block; on the EG the wardrobe goes in front of it, on the OG the laundry closet (O2) beside it.
  • Windows set the partition positions. Window piers on this house are short: 0.85 m beside the OG north and south windows, 0.37 m between the DG south window and the old bath wall. A partition that meets the facade inside a window opening is a construction fault, so every new partition here ends at least 0.15 m clear of a reveal, and the geometry check flags anything closer than 0.12 m.
  • Structure and stacks. Nothing proposed touches an exterior wall, the flue wall, the OG bedroom partition (x 4.18–4.30 m) or the EG spine (x 3.51–3.75 m). The partitions that move are the 0.10–0.12 m ones. All three wet rooms today sit on the west party wall between y 5.0 and 9.1 m, so they share one stack; Option A and E1 keep that, O1 extends it 2.5 m south along the same wall. Only DG Options B and C move the master bath off the stack. Ask the structural engineer to confirm which OG partition, if any, carries the DG north wall at y 1.23 m before you remove anything on the OG; O1 and O2 only add walls there.

How the drawings were checked

Each plan is generated from a list of walls, windows, doors and fixtures in metres. Before rendering, a script tests every drawing: a new partition may not meet a window opening or end within 0.12 m of a reveal; every bath, bedroom, dressing, WC room, corridor and study must have a door on its boundary; every door opening must lie inside a wall; a pocket door's cavity must lie inside a straight wall with no other opening; no fixture may overlap another fixture or a wall; no tall wardrobe may stand in front of a window; no door swing may hit a fixture; and the free depth in front of each WC, basin, shower entry, tub and bed side is measured by ray-casting against walls and fixtures and compared with the VDI 6000 minimum and comfort figures. All six proposals pass; the five faults the script reports are in the current plans. The rendered drawings were then reviewed twice by an independent architectural reviewer against a defect checklist; the first pass found 40 items (among them walls meeting window reveals, pocket cavities crossing wall junctions, a tub standing in front of a door, en-suite doors behind bed heads) and every one was corrected or, where the drawing was right, answered in the captions. The second reviewer pass did not complete (the review service refused the run); the corrections were re-checked by the geometry script and by eye, so that pass remains open.

What this review could not verify

  • Dimensions come from screenshots at ±5 cm. Have the partition positions set out from the as-built survey, not from these numbers.
  • The stair direction was inferred from the arrival-landing logic (a flight cannot arrive flush against the DG north wall). If a flight runs the other way, the only change is that the OG sofa slides 1 m.
  • Roof slopes, parapets and the walkability of the set-back strips are not visible in the plans.
  • The stair is drawn as in your plans, a 2.3–2.5 m straight run, which is too short for a 2.8 m storey; it is kept as a schematic. The flue footprint also differs between your three drawings (0.85 × 1.65 m on the EG, 0.23 × 2.55 m on the OG, 0.24 × 1.6 m on the DG); the proposals keep each as drawn.
  • The research agents could only read search extracts, not full pages; every figure above is tied to the URL that showed it, and VDI 6000-1:2024-07, DIN 18040-2 and Neufert should be checked in the primary text before they go into a tender.
